Privacy Policy

Speed reading - Schulte table for Android
Effective 25 October 2026
The policy for whichever version of the app is current is always at safonov-a.github.io/privacy/schulte-table/android.

In short: your training data stays on your device. The app has no accounts and no advertising, and it uploads none of your results. It sends Google crash reports and usage statistics, tied to a random identifier for this installation of the app rather than to your name or email address. Purchases are made with Google Play. If you have Android backup turned on, your results and settings are also kept in your own Google account.

Which version of the app this describes

This document describes the version of the app that brought you here. Each release links to the document written for it, and that link is fixed in the release, so you are reading about the app you actually have.

If a future release changes what the app stores, backs up or sends, it will not quietly change the meaning of this page: that release gets a document of its own and links there instead, while this one goes on describing the releases it was written for, for as long as they are in use.

Earlier versions of the app did more than this one, and linked to a different policy. What they collected, and what happens to it now, is described under Earlier versions of the app.

Information stored on your device

The app stores the following in its own private storage area on your device, so that it can work and so that your progress is not lost between sessions:

  • Your exercise results — completion times, dates, and which table layout each result belongs to.
  • Your progress through the course and through individual trainings, and the days you trained.
  • Custom tables you create, including the name you give them.
  • Your settings — board size and mode, sound, vibration, font size, whether you have already seen the introduction, and, on Android 12 and older, the language you chose for the app.

The app does not upload this information, synchronise it with any server, share it or sell it. We have no way of reading it. The only copy that leaves your device is the backup described next, which is yours.

Backup and moving to a new device

Android can back up apps to your Google account and copy them to a new phone when you set one up. This app takes part in both, and includes exactly the information listed above: your results and progress, your custom tables, your settings and the language you chose. Nothing else is included — not the analytics and crash-reporting data described below, and not your purchase, which Google Play confirms again on the new device.

The backup is made by Android, not by us, and only if you have backup turned on. It is stored in your Google account, where you can see, turn off and delete it in your device's backup settings and your Google account. We cannot read it. How Google protects backups is described in Google's help pages.

Information sent off the device

The app uses three Google services that receive information from it. Each one is named here with what it receives and why.

Firebase Crashlytics, for crash reports. When the app crashes or records an error, it sends Google a report so that we can find and fix the fault. A report contains the technical details of the error (where in the app's code it happened and what the error was), the time, your device's manufacturer, model and processor type, its Android version and whether it is rooted, the app's version, the device's memory and storage size, the screen orientation, and whether the app was in the foreground. It is tied to identifiers created for this installation of the app — a Crashlytics installation identifier, a Firebase installation ID and a session ID — not to you. Reports contain none of your results, settings or custom tables. Firebase keeps crash reports for 90 days.

Google Analytics for Firebase, for usage statistics. The app does not report anything you do in it; it uses only the events Google Analytics records on its own: when the app is opened for the first time, when a session starts and how long the app is in use, which screens are shown, when the app or Android is updated, when the app's data is cleared or the app is removed, when an in-app purchase is made or refunded, and when the app has an error. With these events Google receives your device's manufacturer, model and type, its Android version and language, the app's version, the store the app was installed from, your approximate location (country, region and city), which Google works out from your IP address, and the app instance ID, a random identifier created for this installation. We use this to know how many people use the app, on which devices and in which languages, and to see how many of them meet a crash. Google Analytics keeps the individual events for two months, and what it holds about an app instance ID for fourteen months after that installation was last used. Summary reports made from this data, which do not identify any installation, are kept longer.

Google Play, for purchases and ratings — see Purchases below. When the app asks you to rate it, the prompt is shown by Google Play; the app is not told whether you rated it or what you wrote.

We use crash reports and usage statistics on the basis of our legitimate interest in keeping the app working and knowing how it is used. The app does not collect the advertising ID, and none of this data is used for advertising.

These services are run by Google, which may process the data outside your country, under its own terms. Google's handling of it is described in the Google Privacy Policy and on Firebase's privacy page.

Apart from these services, the app uses the network only to open a link — this policy, or the app's page on Google Play — which hands the address to your browser or to the Play Store app.

Purchases

The app offers one optional, one-time in-app purchase that unlocks premium features. The purchase is made and paid entirely through Google Play. We never see and never receive your payment card, billing address or Google account details.

To know whether premium is active, the app asks Google Play on your device which purchases you own. The answer is used on the device only; the app sends it nowhere. Google Analytics records that a purchase was made, as one of the events above.

Google's own handling of your purchase is described in the Google Privacy Policy.

What the app does not do

  • No user accounts and no sign-in.
  • No uploading or synchronising of your results, progress or custom tables.
  • No advertising, no ad networks, and no advertising ID.
  • No sale of data, and no sharing of it beyond the Google services named above.
  • No access to your location, contacts, photos, camera, microphone or files. The app asks for no such permission.

Earlier versions of the app

Earlier versions of the app let you sign in with your Google account. If you signed in, Google's Firebase Authentication held your Google account's email address, name, profile photo address and a user identifier, and the app uploaded your exercise results, your course progress and the days you trained to our database on Google's Cloud Firestore, so that they could be restored on another device.

This version has no sign-in and uploads nothing. The data that earlier versions uploaded, and the sign-in records, are kept for six months from the day this version became available on Google Play, and are then deleted.

If you signed in with an earlier version and want your data deleted sooner, write to greenkeyuniverse@gmail.com from the email address of the Google account you signed in with, and we will delete it and your sign-in record.

If you write to us

The app offers a "contact the developer" entry. Choosing it opens your own mail app with a new message addressed to us, pre-filled with the app's version and build number, your Android version, and your interface language — nothing else. Nothing is sent until you send it yourself.

When you do send it, we receive your message and the email address you sent it from, and we use them for one purpose only: to answer you. We keep such correspondence no longer than we need in order to deal with your request, and we do not use it for anything else.

Links to other services

The app can open this policy in your browser and the app's page on Google Play. Those are operated by others, under their own privacy policies. We are not responsible for their content or their practices.

Children

This app is not directed at children under 13. It asks no one for personal information, and the crash reports and usage statistics described above are collected in the same way for everyone, without the app knowing anyone's age.

Your rights

Privacy law in your region, including the General Data Protection Regulation in the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom, gives you rights over personal data a company holds about you: to see it, correct it, delete it, restrict its use, and object to its use. You can also complain to the data protection authority where you live.

Crash reports and usage statistics are tied only to random identifiers for your installation of the app, which the app does not show, so we cannot pick out which of them are yours. They are deleted after the periods given above. Clearing the app's data or reinstalling it gives the app new identifiers, and nothing it sends afterwards can be linked to what it sent before.

Deleting your data

  • On your device: uninstall the app, or clear its storage in Android's settings (usually Settings → Apps → Speed reading - Schulte table → Storage → Clear storage). Your results, progress, custom tables and settings are gone.
  • In your Google backup: uninstalling the app does not remove its backup, and reinstalling it brings your data back. To remove the backup as well, delete it in your device's backup settings or in your Google account's backups, as described in Google's help pages.
  • Data we hold: data uploaded by earlier versions, and email you sent us. Write to greenkeyuniverse@gmail.com and we will delete it — for earlier versions' data, from the address you signed in with, as described above.

Your purchase is held by Google Play against your Google account, not by us, and is restored if you install the app again.
